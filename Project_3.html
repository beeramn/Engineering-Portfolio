SegmentAnyBone

A research model designed to segment bones across MRI exams from different body locations and sequences. SegmentAnyBone adapts the Segment Anything Model (SAM) with a 3D depth-attention branch and supports both automatic and prompt-based segmentation.

Project Overview

The Problem

Bone segmentation in MRI is difficult to automate across anatomy and scan protocols. MRI bone annotations are scarce, and nearby soft tissue, water-fat boundaries, and low-contrast regions can make bone edges ambiguous. Existing approaches often focus on one body location or sequence.

Research Goal

SegmentAnyBone aims to provide one adaptable model for bone segmentation across common MRI locations and sequences. The model predicts a binary mask for bone versus non-bone, rather than classifying each individual bone. It can segment all visible bones automatically or use point and box prompts to target selected regions and correct missed areas.

My Involvement

My hands-on contribution focused on annotating MRI data for the project. I was also involved in and informed about how the labeled data was used in model training. The model and training sections below describe the broader team approach presented in the paper.

Annotated MRI Dataset

The paper describes a multi-tier annotation effort that built a broad dataset for a task with few existing public bone MRI labels. The project reports more than 300 annotated volumes spanning 17 body locations.

Annotation Scope

The initial annotation effort prioritized T1-weighted MRI and five primary locations: hip, shoulder, humerus, knee, and thigh. Annotation expanded to additional locations. The goal was to label every visible bone, including bones that appeared incidentally in a scan, resulting in 96 unique bone types.

Annotation Review

The annotation process was guided by a fellowship-trained musculoskeletal radiologist and a senior orthopedic surgery fellow. Experienced physicians oversaw annotator training, refinement, and approval of many annotations; test cases were reviewed by an experienced physician.

Extending to Other Sequences

To include non-T1 scans without repeating all manual labeling, the team paired same-patient volumes from the same view. A research annotator manually checked alignment, and T1 masks were transferred only when the visible skeletal structures aligned well.

Patient-Level Evaluation Splits

The development data used separate patients across training and validation sets. The paper describes 195 training volumes, validation sets of 44 and 32 volumes, and an independent test set of 35 volumes across seven locations, with no patient overlap.

Annotation quality and coverage were central to the project: the model needed examples across many bones, locations, and MRI sequences to learn a general bone-versus-background task.

SAM Adaptation and Depth Attention

SegmentAnyBone combines a slice-based SAM branch with a lightweight 3D attention branch. A learnable gate fuses the image features with information from neighboring slices.

2D SAM Branch

Each MRI slice is resized to the SAM input resolution and processed by a vision-transformer image encoder, prompt encoder, and mask decoder. The output is adapted for binary bone segmentation.

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning

Adapter blocks are added to attention blocks in the image encoder and mask decoder. Training updates a small part of the pretrained model, leaving most pretrained parameters frozen during adapter fine-tuning.

3D Depth-Attention Branch

A low-resolution 3D branch uses a streamlined V-Net-style network to estimate where bone is likely to appear through the volume. It draws on adjacent-slice context, then the learnable gate blends that information into the slice-level image features.

Hybrid Prompts

Training alternates between prompted and automatic segmentation. In the paper's setup, 30% of batch iterations use generated point or box prompts, while the remaining 70% omit prompts and predict all bone regions. At inference, users can add prompts to focus or correct a result.

Two-Stage Training Strategy

  1. Adapt the model: fine-tune SAM adapters for automatic segmentation and train the 3D attention branch separately.
  2. Fuse context: incorporate hybrid prompting and attention fusion, freeze the image encoder, and train the fusion layers and mask decoder.

This keeps the 2D branch useful on its own while allowing the final model to combine slice features with volume-level context.

Evaluation and Generalization

The study compared SegmentAnyBone with standard CNN and transformer segmentation approaches. The main comparison evaluates T1 test cases across seven body locations; additional experiments test new locations, MRI sequences, and an external lumbar-spine dataset.

Unseen Locations

Training on five primary locations and testing on unseen locations produced a reported average DSC of 78.73%.

Unseen Sequences

Training exclusively with T1 sequences and testing on other MRI sequences produced a reported DSC of 77.68%.

External Dataset

On the external MRI-LumbarSpine dataset, SegmentAnyBone achieved a reported DSC of 77.62%.

Interactive Correction

Point and box prompts can target selected bones or correct an automatic prediction, including small regions missed near the edge of a volume.

Scope and Limitations

Binary Segmentation

The current model predicts bone versus non-bone. It does not assign a separate class to each bone; bone classification is described as a possible extension.

Evaluation Coverage

The main test results cover seven body locations. The paper identifies limited expert annotation time as a constraint on the number of locations that could be tested.

Research Context

The results demonstrate segmentation performance in the study datasets. They are research findings and do not by themselves establish clinical deployment or diagnostic use.
